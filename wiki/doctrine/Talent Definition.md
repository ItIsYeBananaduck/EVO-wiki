# Talent Definition
## Purpose

Define Talents as preserved, governed execution patterns that reduce repeated reasoning while maintaining strict control and bounded behavior.

---

## Core Principle

> A Talent is a trusted execution pattern, not a permission grant.

Talents exist to:

- reduce repeated inference
- preserve proven execution patterns
- enable efficient reuse of known-safe behavior

Talents do not:

- grant authority
- bypass governance
- expand scope implicitly

---

## Definition

A **Talent** is a preserved, repeatable, and governed execution pattern derived from validated Methods or verified Task Chain participation.

A Talent represents:

- a concrete execution structure
- a bounded scope of operation
- a trusted pattern of behavior

This means:

- they cannot be modified silently
- they cannot evolve implicitly
- they cannot expand scope without validation

All changes must occur through:

- validated variation (branching)
- or new Talent creation

---

## Creation Paths

### 1. Method Promotion (Bottom-Up)

A Method becomes a Talent when:

- it has been validated through repeated successful execution
- it meets promotion requirements
- the user explicitly chooses to preserve it

This path prioritizes:

- user control
- explicit trust decisions

---

### 2. Task Chain Verification (Top-Down)

A Method may become a Talent when:

- it participates in a Task Chain that becomes **Verified**
- the Task Chain completes 3 successful validated runs
- the Method remains stable within the locked chain structure

This path:

- promotes proven execution through structure
- reduces redundant validation effort

> Task Chains verify as whole structures.

> Talents inherit proof through participation.

### User-Created Immediate Promotion

In certain cases, user-created Talents may be immediately preserved when:

- the structure is explicitly defined
- the user confirms intent
- the scope is clearly bounded

Even in these cases:

- the Talent remains subject to governance
- variation must still validate separately

---

## Governance

Talents are always governed based on their execution surface.

### App Talents (Closed Domains)

- execute within EVOmind and EVOtraining
- governed by application logic
- do not require Delegator

### External Talents

- execute on external or shared systems
- governed by Delegator
- require explicit approval where applicable

> Talents never grant execution authority.

> Governance is always enforced externally or internally.

---

## Stability Rule

A Talent is:

- preserved
- bounded
- non-mutating

This means:

- it cannot evolve silently
- it cannot expand scope
- it cannot inherit new authority

All changes must go through:

- Method validation
- or Task Chain verification

---

## Variation Model

Talents support controlled variation through validated branching.

A variation may be incorporated into a Talent only when:

- the same target is preserved
- the same step is modified
- only one part of the method changes
- the variation is validated through 3 successful runs

When validated:

- the variation becomes a bounded branch within the Talent

If these conditions are not met:

- the variation becomes a **new Talent**

> Variation is validated like a Method.

> It is incorporated like a Talent branch.

---

## Composition

Talents may be composed within:

- Methods
- Task Chains
- other Talents

Composition:

- reduces repeated inference
- reduces repeated approval where safe
- must preserve scope and authority boundaries

Composition must not create:

- implicit authority transfer
- scope expansion
- execution ambiguity

---

## Relationship to Task Chains

Task Chains:

- represent multi-step execution structures
- may contain Talents

When a Task Chain becomes Verified:

- Talents remain unchanged
- non-Talent Methods are promoted

Talents improve Task Chain execution by:

- reducing reasoning overhead
- stabilizing repeated patterns

---

## Relationship to Inference Compression

Talents are a form of **inference compression**.

They:

- eliminate repeated reasoning for known patterns
- preserve trusted execution paths
- enable efficient reuse of proven behavior

This is only valid when:

- execution is explicit
- patterns are stable
- governance remains intact

---

## No Toggle Rule

Talents cannot be enabled or disabled dynamically.

They exist as:

- preserved capabilities
- callable structures

Usage is controlled through:

- invocation
- task selection
- or user intent

Not through internal toggling mechanisms.

---

## Final Principle

> Preserve proven execution.

> Bound all behavior.

> Never trade control for convenience.

Talents make execution faster.

They do not make execution less safe.
